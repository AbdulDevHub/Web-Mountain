The town’s clock tower rang thirteen times, and Mara woke with a gasp.

That was strange enough—everyone knew the tower had been broken for years—but stranger still was the faint glow tracing the lines of her hands. Thin threads of light pulsed beneath her skin, like constellations rearranging themselves.

She sat up slowly. The room looked the same: cracked ceiling, the old bookshelf listing to the left, rain tapping at the window. Yet something had shifted, as if the air itself were holding its breath.

When Mara stood, the floorboards whispered her name.

She stumbled back, heart racing. “I’m dreaming,” she said, but the words felt weak. The glow in her hands brightened, responding to her fear, and the whispering stopped.

A memory surfaced—one that wasn’t hers. People standing in circles. The clock tower whole and gleaming. A voice saying, *Sleep now. Wake when it’s time.*

Outside, the clock tower rang again. Once.

Mara stepped toward the window. The rain hung frozen in the air, each drop a tiny mirror reflecting her face—calm now, focused, awake.

She understood then: the town had been waiting, and so had she.

Mara closed her glowing hands into fists, and the world began to move again.
